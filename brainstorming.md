## Step 2: Evaluate App Ideas

After discussing the ideas and voting as a group, we selected three concepts for further evaluation:

1. PawPal - Puppy Care Tracker
2. TriviaClash - Multiplayer Trivia
3. ParkSpot - Parking Location Tracker

Each idea was evaluated using the Mobile, Story, Market, Habit, and Scope criteria. We also considered API availability and technical complexity.

### Idea 1: PawPal - Puppy Care Tracker

**Mobile:** PawPal is well suited for mobile devices because puppy owners need quick access to their pets' routines throughout the day. Users can record feeding times, potty breaks, grooming activities, and other care tasks directly from their phones.

**Story:** New puppy owners often struggle to maintain consistent routines while managing responsibilities such as feeding, house training, and grooming. PawPal provides an organized place to record these activities and monitor completed tasks.

**Market:** The target audience includes new puppy owners, first-time pet owners, and households that want to organize daily pet care. Existing pet care apps demonstrate interest in this category, while PawPal would focus on a simple experience for puppy owners.

**Habit:** The app encourages frequent use because puppy care involves multiple activities throughout the day. Users may open PawPal several times daily to review tasks and record completed activities.

**Scope:** PawPal has a manageable scope for a student capstone project. The initial version can focus on a puppy profile, activity tracking, completion status, and activity history. Features such as reminders, weight tracking, and sharing can be added later if time allows.

**API Availability:** The core features can be implemented using SwiftUI and local data persistence without requiring a third-party API. Apple's Photos framework and notification APIs may support optional features.

### Idea 2: TriviaClash - Multiplayer Trivia

**Mobile:** TriviaClash is suitable for mobile devices because users can participate in short trivia matches from anywhere. Touch controls make selecting answers simple and intuitive.

**Story:** TriviaClash provides an entertaining way for users to test their knowledge and compete with friends or other players through trivia challenges.

**Market:** The target audience includes students, casual gamers, and people who enjoy quizzes or competitive games. Trivia applications are already popular, so the app would need engaging features to stand out.

**Habit:** Users could return to play additional rounds, compete with friends, or improve their scores. New questions and challenges could encourage repeated use.

**Scope:** A basic single-player trivia application would be manageable, but implementing real-time multiplayer features would significantly increase complexity. User accounts, game synchronization, and matchmaking could make the project more difficult within the available time.

**API Availability:** Public trivia question APIs, such as Open Trivia Database, could provide questions and answer choices. Real-time multiplayer would require additional backend services.

### Idea 3: ParkSpot - Parking Location Tracker

**Mobile:** ParkSpot benefits from mobile capabilities such as GPS and maps. Users could save their parking location and receive directions back to their vehicle.

**Story:** Drivers sometimes forget where they parked, especially in large parking lots, unfamiliar neighborhoods, or busy urban areas. ParkSpot would provide a convenient way to save and find their parking location.

**Market:** The target audience includes drivers, commuters, students, travelers, and people who frequently park in unfamiliar places. The app could be useful, although similar functionality is available in some navigation applications.

**Habit:** Users would primarily open ParkSpot when parking in an unfamiliar location. The frequency of use would depend on their driving and parking habits.

**Scope:** A basic version could be completed using location services and a map interface. Additional features, such as parking reminders, photos, and location history, could be implemented if time allows.

**API Availability:** Apple's Core Location and MapKit frameworks could provide location tracking, map display, and navigation features without requiring an external third-party API.

## Final Idea: PawPal

After considering our group discussion, voting results, technical requirements, and the app evaluation criteria, we selected **PawPal - Puppy Care Tracker** as our capstone project.

PawPal received the strongest support during our group vote, and we believe it offers a good balance of usefulness, mobile functionality, and manageable development scope.

The app addresses a practical problem for new puppy owners by helping them organize daily responsibilities such as feeding, potty breaks, and grooming.

Our initial goal is to develop an iOS application where users can create a puppy profile, add and manage care activities, mark activities as completed, and review their activity history.

Optional features such as profile pictures, notifications, weight tracking, and shared caregiving information may be considered after the required features are implemented.

We selected PawPal because it has a clear target audience, encourages repeated daily use, and provides an opportunity to apply the SwiftUI skills learned throughout the course.
